Take back the street, for one evening

People need ways to assert and explore their right to privacy.

The Privacy Parade invites you to wander around Finsbury Park in full disguise, so you can't be tracked by cameras, facial recognition and the other technologies that quietly take our data as we go about our lives.

It's a gentle, Halloween-themed walk of conscientious objectors to surveillance. We'll wear garments chosen to keep our faces our own, and carry signs and leaflets for the people who see us pass.

It's fun and experimental, and the message is serious.

Surveillance is growing. Nobody asked us.

It's spreading through our streets, our supermarkets and our homes. It puts our data at risk, rarely gets properly justified, costs a fortune, and leaves us a less trusting society.

A recipe for any high street

The Privacy Parade is a model any group or community can pick up: a way to exercise the right to privacy in a playful, performative way, and to reflect on what our high streets have become.

  • GatherMeet somewhere friendly (ours is SPACE4) and get into disguise together.
  • StewardPair the sheeted with the sighted. Agree a short, simple route.
  • WanderWalk slowly past the cameras. Signs and leaflets explain why.
  • ReflectCome back, unmask, and talk about how it felt to go unseen.

This is a work in progress. Suggestions, improvements and local customisations are welcome.
